# Part 1 analysis plan

## Sources and eligibility

- **GSS.** Use the NORC 1993 single-year Stata archive from the [official data page](https://gss.norc.org/get-the-data/stata.html), with the [NORC codebook and questionnaires](https://gss.norc.org/get-documentation.html) and the archive's Stata labels. Select year 1993, retain one row per unique respondent ID, and sort by ID. The 18-item music battery occurs on forms A, B, and C. The archive's expected SHA-256 is `98c828bbdb20b02ae0efc067a2f24cf1afa87aaa012d3c97008901359b04e68`.
- Recode `HAPPY` to 1 = not too happy, 2 = pretty happy, 3 = very happy. Genres in questionnaire order are `BIGBAND, BLUGRASS, COUNTRY, BLUES, MUSICALS, CLASSICL, FOLK, GOSPEL, JAZZ, LATIN, MOODEASY, NEWAGE, OPERA, RAP, REGGAE, CONROCK, OLDIES, HVYMETAL`. Reverse valid genre codes 1 through 5 so 5 means "like very much". Treat unfamiliarity, nonresponse, and inapplicability as missing.
- Check and report missingness separately for every candidate GSS control, denominated by the entire 1993 wave. Exclude a control from the primary model for strictly more than 30% design missingness, using Stata design missing codes and the documented ballot routing for `HEALTH`. Candidate controls are age (linear and quadratic), sex, race, education (years), family income (linear in `log1p(REALINC)`), marital status, health, work status, and number of children. Use factor indicators for sex, race, marital, health and work status. `HEALTH` was omitted from ballot B, with about one third ineligible according to the 1993 documentation. Use the surviving controls even when individual nonresponse reduces sample size.
- **Young People Survey.** Read the publisher-supplied `columns.csv` dictionary before opening `responses.csv`. Validate column order, labels and scales against the dictionary and [publisher's dataset description](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/miroslavsabo/young-people-survey). Use the ordered 1–5 `Happiness in life` item, with 5 as the highest outcome. Use the music genre and film genre batteries, omitting `Music`, `Movies` (both global enjoyment items) and `Slow songs or fast songs` (tempo preference). Use the complete hobbies and interests battery, including interests in school subjects, and report the item lists and response prompts. Use age (linear and quadratic), gender, education, village/town and number of siblings as controls, using factor indicators for gender, education and village/town. Report any documented unavailable fields. Use CSV row order as the respondent identifier, with one row per submitted response.
- Use complete cases for outcome and retained controls. Require valid responses for at least half the items, rounding up, to compute a score. Each Slovak domain uses its own eligibility, and the combined score requires each of the three domains to be eligible. Report the eligible count, complete-case count, and missingness. Do not remove cases based on their realized outcome category or choose specifications based on results.

## Scores, models, and checks

- The liking score is the person's mean over valid 1–5 ratings. The combined Slovak score averages the available items across the three domains with equal weight per item. Also compute counts of ratings 1–2 and of ratings 5, valid-item count, and population SD of ratings within each person. Summarize these components and run one response-style sensitivity adding the share of answers at either endpoint (1 or 5), within-person SD, and share of items answered to the primary model.
- Fit proportional-odds logistic regression on the complete analytical sample. Standardize liking to mean zero and population SD one within that sample. Report the slope and 95% Wald CI, cumulative odds ratio per SD, and the average predicted percentage-point change for the top outcome from −0.5 SD to +0.5 SD holding each respondent's controls fixed. Use unweighted respondent-level models and model-based intervals. For GSS, also report a WTSSPS-weighted binary logit estimate for very happy as a sensitivity if positive weights are available, using its own complete weight cases and primary-sample score scaling. The primary estimand is the conditional association in the analyzed respondents.
- Use seed **41393**. Assign the two halves once per survey by permuting the sorted survey IDs with NumPy's default RNG, with the first floor(N/2) IDs assigned to training and the remainder to testing. Assign membership before analysis exclusions and carry it across all domains. Fit controls-only and liking-plus-controls ordinal models in training, predict probability of the highest outcome on the same held-out rows, and report both ROC AUCs and their difference. Learn scaling and categorical levels from training. Pool any unseen test factor level into the training reference level and report such cases.
- Test H1 with 10,000 two-sided permutations. Fit the controls-only ordinal model and calculate each respondent's log-likelihood derivative with respect to their linear predictor. Regress standardized liking on the controls with an intercept. Calculate the inner product of the resulting liking residuals with the ordinal score, then repeat after independently shuffling the liking residuals while keeping controls and happiness paired. Report the plus-one Monte Carlo p-value against the absolute observed statistic. This is an approximate test requiring exchangeability of the residuals.
- Residualize liking on the same age and education terms used in the ordinal model and rerun with the remaining specification unchanged, standardizing the residual. Report adjusted per-SD slopes alongside the raw-score model, and unadjusted Pearson associations with numeric ordered happiness for raw and residualized liking. The adjusted fits are reparameterizations because both contain the same age and education terms.
- **GSS matched check.** Use complete primary cases from the ballots eligible for health and with observed health. Form empirical income and liking terciles in this subset, assigning ties at a quantile cut to the lower tercile. Define strata using income tercile × exact marital status × exact health × exact work status. Compare the percentage very happy in top versus bottom global liking terciles within strata containing both. Pool differences using weights `n_top * n_bottom / (n_top + n_bottom)`. Report retained strata, extreme respondents, and the corresponding share of eligible extreme respondents. For the three-group display, use strata containing all three terciles and common weights proportional to the harmonic mean of the three cell sizes. If no strata overlap, report the comparison as unavailable. Also refit the primary ordinal model on the health-complete cases with and without health to distinguish case selection from adding the control.

## Short quiz and replication

- Use identical respondent rows for all quiz lengths within each analysis. Fill quiz-only gaps with each item's observed training mean, applying that mean to both halves. The general liking score in the main model uses observed ratings only. For quiz scoring, take the mean across selected items. Drop an item from the quiz only if it has no valid training rating, and report if this occurs.
- Use the signed control-adjusted Pearson correlation with the numeric ordered outcome. Regress numeric happiness and each imputed item on controls with an intercept in training, then apply the fitted nuisance coefficients to the test rows. Starting at zero items, append the item giving the largest signed training correlation of the selected mean with happiness, resolving ties by source order. For the cross-check, rank by absolute coefficient from a Lasso model on standard-scaled residualized training items, with alpha selected by five shuffled training-only folds using seed 41393. Evaluate equal-weight means along both orders on the untouched outcome and item residuals in the test half.
- Plot both test curves and report the smallest greedy k whose test association reaches 90% of the full battery's positive test association, and list its items. If the full association is zero or negative, the 90% target is undefined. Use a 2,000-draw paired test-row percentile bootstrap for the full-score association, holding the training procedure fixed. If the full association is below 0.10 or its interval includes zero, describe a threshold crossing as an observed, exploratory result with weak support. Selection of k scans the test curve, so k requires independent validation.
- Repeat the core association, AUC, permutation, residualization, response-style and quiz analyses for Slovak music, film, hobbies and interests, and combined. Treat the combined estimate as the main replication and the three domain estimates as descriptive. Report whether the direction and uncertainty support replication, including the difference in outcome wording and population. There is no Slovak counterpart to the GSS matched strata because the requisite circumstances were not measured comparably.

# Part 2 analysis plan

## Participant and session protocol

- Run an owner-only pilot of 14 Indianapolis calendar days, beginning on the first day with a started chat. The day number is fixed by the date. Missed dates stay missing. The app runs on loopback only, with one chat per date. The chat ends at eight user/model exchanges or 150 seconds. A session that ends over an immediate safety concern stops without requesting ratings or reader guesses. Retain counts of started, completed, crisis-ended, and missing sessions for troubleshooting.
- Begin with consent and a random access code. Store transcripts, message times, ratings, life satisfaction, and separately generated guesses in local SQLite. Send conversation text to Anthropic for chat and prediction, with fixed instructions and topics. Never send ratings, anchor responses, the participant code, or local timestamps. Disclose Anthropic's 30-day retention for Fable 5.2, which is separate from deletion on this machine.
- Use a fixed opening question and a six-topic pool covering music, science curiosities, time with friends, everyday food, weather, and weekend activities. Inspired by Part 1, the first three topics include interests in music, science, and socializing. Draw three topics for each day with seed 41393 and complementary adjacent-day sets, so each occurs seven times across the scheduled 14 days. Set order in advance. Ask about neutral preferences. Treat any preference cue as a candidate predictor to be tested.
- Ask “How are you feeling right now?” on a separate screen, using integer ratings from 0 through 10. On days 1 and 14 also ask “Overall, how satisfied are you with your life nowadays?” on a 0 to 10 scale. Treat the two life-satisfaction ratings descriptively.
- Launch two fresh requests after the chat. The cold reader sees today's transcript only. The history reader sees today's transcript and earlier completed chat transcripts. Neither gets ratings or guesses from any day. Each returns a 0–10 numeric guess, confidence from 0 to 1 interpreted as the probability of being within one rating point, and short textual cues. Store invalid responses and failed requests as missing guesses. Reveal guesses at pilot close, once day 14 is complete or its calendar date has passed.
- Use `claude-fable-5-2` in both roles at low effort and verify the exact ID with the authenticated Models API before starting a live pilot. The pilot call limits are 160 output tokens per chat response and 320 per reader response. Count thinking tokens in those limits. Use the standard endpoint and no paid tools, caching, fast mode, or automatic retry.
- Pilot API spend is capped at $10, with actual message usage recorded in an anonymous local ledger. Reserve against the model's documented maximum input context and request output limit before each Messages call. Release the unused reservation on verified usage and retain the reservation on unresolved failures. This errs on the side of stopping early if available headroom falls below the worst-case cost of one request.

## Outcomes, comparisons, and pilot reporting

- **Primary reader:** cold. Pair each guess with that day's independent rating. For each participant with at least three pairs and variation in both variables, compute Pearson's r. Average Fisher-transformed correlations with equal participant weight and transform back to r. For the eventual study, compute a two-sided Monte Carlo p-value using 10,000 independent day shuffles of ratings within each participant and the plus-one correction. Use seed 41393. Report excluded participants and days. Interpret the test subject to exchangeability of days under the null.
- Compare guesses with the person's leave-one-out mean, the rating on the immediately preceding calendar day, and a no-LLM Ridge model with fixed penalty and standardized features for message length, reply time, and a fixed English sentiment lexicon. Fit the text baseline with strictly earlier rated sessions in the owner pilot and held-out participants for the eventual study. The mean baseline is a retrospective benchmark. Compare mean absolute errors on common eligible days, disclosing the number of paired days. Also compare cold and history on their common rated days.
- Summarize confidence against the fraction within one point in fixed bins, with counts. The eventual study also fits a model with a random intercept for each person and within-person-centered guesses. Report life satisfaction descriptively and show any missingness.
- For the 14-day owner pilot, report the observed correlation and errors as debugging summaries, alongside completion, model errors, rating availability, and token spend. Show the number of observations and omit inferential claims. Use pilot logs to estimate API cost for 8 and 12 participants and propose a cap before Gate 2. The study endpoint concerns tracking a participant's own daily variation.
